## About

I am a computer science graduate based in Portland, Oregon, with experience across software development, data systems, community-based research, and hands-on operations work.

My background is intentionally cross-disciplinary. I have technical training in systems programming, data structures, SQL databases, web development, and AI-assisted workflows, alongside years of experience in community organizing, housing equity, grant-supported research, hospitality operations, and team coordination. I am especially interested in work where technology has to meet real human context: tools that make messy workflows clearer, data systems that support accountability, and products that help people do meaningful work with less friction.

Through Future Generations Collaborative, I contributed to community-based research, grant reporting support, outreach, and education work focused on Indigenous data sovereignty, housing equity, tenant protections, and anti-displacement. That work shaped how I think about data: not just as information to organize, but as something that carries responsibility, context, and community impact.

In hospitality operations, I have managed fast-moving service environments, coordinated staff, handled accounting and cash management, supported events, and made practical inventory decisions around demand, shelf life, storage limits, and waste. That firsthand operational experience now informs how I approach software: I like building tools that respect the complexity of real work instead of pretending everything is already clean and structured.

Recently, I built an AI-assisted bar inventory workflow that turns messy spoken inventory notes into structured inventory data. The project combines TypeScript, React, Python, SQL, voice-to-data workflows, command-driven parsing, and practical knowledge of bar operations. It reflects the kind of work I want to keep doing: building useful systems at the intersection of software, data, operations, and people.
